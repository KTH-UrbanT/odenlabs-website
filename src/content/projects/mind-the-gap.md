Swedish energy performance certificates cover most of the building stock and, unlike in other EU countries, record measured rather than calculated energy use. This makes them a unique data resource. They are now also used as policy instruments, for minimum energy performance standards and the national building renovation plan, a role they were not designed for. The data lacks continuity between certification rounds, is hard to combine with other data that transition work needs, and is not accessible to every actor with a transition mandate.

Two doctoral projects address this gap. One, at Malmö University and RISE, develops methods to harmonise building energy data over time and enrich it with machine learning. The other, at KTH, maps mismatches between actors' mandates, data access and policy instruments, and studies how polycentric governance of building energy data can support coordination across scales. Joint participatory backcasting workshops with stakeholders bring the two together into a shared vision of Sweden's building energy data infrastructure in 2045.

Swedish title: "Se upp för glappet, dataanvändningsglappet". The project is part of the Graduate School in Energy Systems (FoES).

Project leader: [Tove Malmqvist Stigell](https://www.kth.se/profile/tovem), KTH. Partners: KTH, Malmö University and RISE Research Institutes of Sweden.
